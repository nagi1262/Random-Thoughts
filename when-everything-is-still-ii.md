# When Everything Is Still - II

I kept thinking about that last night.

If something remains, how would you know what it means?

A photograph can tell you what someone looked like.

A name can tell you who they were.

An address can tell you where to find them.

But none of those things tell you what they wanted you to find.

Maybe that is the strange thing about leaving something behind.

You don't get to explain it.

The person who finds it has to decide what it means.

I've always liked that idea.

A message does not need to say everything.

Sometimes the important part is the thing it makes you notice.

A word repeated twice.

A name that seems slightly out of place.

A sentence that feels like it belongs somewhere else.

Even a piece of writing can hide another meaning inside it.

We tend to read words from left to right and assume that is the only
direction they can go.

Maybe it isn't.

There are old ways of hiding things in plain sight.

Take a message.

Choose a key.

Let one alphabet move against another.

The letters change, but the message stays there underneath.

Someone who doesn't know what to look for sees nonsense.

Someone who has the key sees a sentence.

I like that.

The idea that the secret was never really gone.

It was simply written in a language the wrong person didn't know how to
read.

And a key is rarely something you invent.

More often, it is something you have already been given without
realizing it.

Sometimes you just have to look back at what you already know.

A name.

A place.

An address.

Even the place a message belongs to can say more than the message
itself.

Perhaps that is why we give things addresses in the first place.

So they can be found again.

Maybe that is true of everything we leave behind.

Maybe the right question isn't whether something survived.

Maybe the right question is whether someone will recognize it.

Because if a soul can leave a mark without being seen,

and a message can remain hidden without being lost,

then perhaps nothing needs to disappear completely.

It only needs someone willing to look closely enough.

And if you ever find something of mine that makes no sense,

don't assume it is meaningless.

Sometimes the thing that looks wrong is the thing asking to be read
differently.

# Things We Never Say

There are things people say every day without thinking.

Hello.

Take care.

See you tomorrow.

And then there are the things we think about saying for years.

I should have stayed.

I was wrong.

I miss you.

I'm sorry.

Thank you.

Somehow, those words always seem heavier.

Maybe because saying them means admitting that something matters.

Silence is easier.

You can hide inside it.

You can convince yourself that there will be another opportunity,
another conversation, another evening when the words will finally
find their way out.

Most of the time, there is.

Sometimes there isn't.

I've always found it strange that we are more afraid of a few seconds
of discomfort than we are of years of wondering what might have
happened if we had spoken.

We rehearse conversations that will never happen.

We imagine different endings.

We think of better answers while walking home, hours after the
conversation has already ended.

And somehow, in our imagination, we are always brave enough to say
everything.

Reality is rarely that kind.

There are people I have known for years whose most important thoughts
I've never heard.

Perhaps they have never heard mine either.

We can spend an enormous amount of time with someone and still leave
entire rooms of ourselves unexplored.

I don't think that is necessarily a failure.

People are not books that owe us every page.

Some things belong to silence.

Some memories are safer when they remain unnamed.

Some feelings become smaller once you give them a word.

But there are other things that silence doesn't protect.

An apology left unsaid does not stop being necessary.

Gratitude does not become less true because it was never spoken.

And missing someone doesn't become easier simply because you never
admit it.

Maybe the things we never say don't really disappear.

They change shape.

They become the pauses between sentences.

The messages we type and delete.

The calls we almost make.

The places we avoid because they remember too much.

Perhaps that is why silence can feel so loud.

It is full of everything that almost existed.

I don't think we need to say everything.

That would be impossible.

But sometimes I wonder how different our lives would be if we were
just a little less afraid of saying the things that matter.

Not everything needs an answer.

Some words only need to be heard once.

And sometimes, once they are finally spoken, you realize the silence
was never protecting you from anything.

It was only keeping the door closed.

# The Last Ordinary Day

There is probably a last time for everything.

The last time you walk through a particular door without knowing you won't
come back.

The last conversation with someone before the two of you slowly become
strangers.

The last evening you spend somewhere that, at the time, feels completely
ordinary.

The strange part is that nobody tells you.

There is no announcement.

Nothing pauses to give the moment the importance it deserves.

The sun doesn't look different. The clock doesn't hesitate. The person
sitting across from you doesn't suddenly become a memory.

Everything continues exactly as it always has.

And then, sometime later, you remember that day and realize it was the
last.

I think that's what makes ordinary moments so difficult to appreciate.

We are always waiting for something worth remembering.

A birthday.

A journey.

A goodbye.

A beginning.

We take photographs when the sky turns orange, when everyone is smiling,
when something important is happening.

But nobody takes a photograph of the Tuesday afternoon when nothing
happened.

Nobody writes down the conversation about what to eat for dinner.

Nobody remembers the exact sound of a friend's laugh on a day when there
was no reason to think they would ever stop being around.

Yet those might be the moments we miss the most.

Perhaps memory is unfair that way.

It doesn't preserve things according to their importance.

It preserves fragments.

A particular smell.

The sound of rain against a window.

A song you haven't heard in years.

The way sunlight used to fall across a room.

A sentence someone said without thinking.

Sometimes I wonder how many important moments I've already lived through
without recognizing them.

How many ordinary afternoons have quietly crossed the line between
"today" and "once."

Maybe that's why I have started paying more attention to things that
don't seem important.

The walk home.

The five minutes before everyone leaves.

The conversations that have no purpose.

The places I pass every day without looking closely.

Not because I think every moment needs to be remembered.

But because one day, some completely unremarkable moment will become
important simply because it was the last time it happened.

And I won't know which one it was.

None of us ever do.

Maybe that's okay.

Maybe a moment doesn't need to know that it is precious in order to be
precious.

Maybe some things are allowed to matter only after they are gone.

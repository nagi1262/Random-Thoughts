# The Things We Outgrow

I used to think growing up meant becoming someone new.

A better version, perhaps. Someone more certain, more capable of
answering questions, someone who could look back at the person they
used to be and understand why he made all those mistakes.

But I don't think that's what happens.

I think growing up is mostly learning how to live beside the people
you used to be.

There is the child who believed every promise was permanent.
The teenager who thought silence was a form of strength.
The person who mistook being needed for being loved.
The person who stayed too long because leaving felt like failure.

They are all still here, in small and inconvenient ways.

Sometimes they appear in the things I avoid.
Sometimes in the things I desire.
Sometimes in the way I react to a sentence that someone else has
already forgotten saying.

It is strange how much of a person can survive without being noticed.

We change our clothes, our opinions, our handwriting.
We move to different places, learn different words, meet different
people.

Yet some invisible part of us continues to make the same choices,
as if it never received the news that we had moved on.

Perhaps that is why old photographs feel so uncomfortable.

They don't simply show us what we looked like.
They show us a version of ourselves that existed without our
permission, a person who had hopes we no longer remember having.

We look at them and search for evidence of who we are now.

But the photograph doesn't explain anything.

It only asks us to accept that both people are real.

I wonder if change is less about leaving things behind and more about
making room for them.

The past does not always disappear when we stop speaking about it.
Sometimes it becomes quieter. Sometimes it learns to live beneath
ordinary days.

And sometimes, without warning, we discover that the person we were
trying so hard to become has been waiting patiently inside the person
we already are.

Maybe that is what growing up means.

Not becoming someone else.

Just learning to forgive the versions of yourself that could not yet
understand what you know now.
